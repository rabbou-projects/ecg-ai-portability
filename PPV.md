# A model's PPV, recomputed for a new hospital

The measurement of the positive predictive value recomputed by Bayes' theorem against the one observed, across transfers between hospitals, care settings and corpora, is part of the article [REPORT.md](REPORT.md): sections 2.5 and 3.1 for the measurement, 3.5 for the predictions that need no diagnosis, and 3.6 for the three repairs judged on net benefit. The methods in statistical terms are in Part 3 of [SUPPLEMENT.md](SUPPLEMENT.md).

## Local labels needed to correct an ECG model's positive predictive value at a new hospital, and the cost in sensitivity

### Objective

An ECG model is given to a hospital with a positive predictive value (PPV), the share of the patients it flags who have the disease. That PPV was measured at the developer's site. It depends on how common the disease is among the patients tested, the prevalence, so at another hospital the figure does not hold. The hospital can confirm the diagnosis of some of its own patients, which gives local labels, and use them to correct the figure. This study asks how many local labels are needed before the PPV a hospital states for the model matches the PPV it then observes, and how many cases the model misses once the PPV is corrected.

A model flags a patient when its score passes a threshold. At a fixed threshold, the PPV moves with prevalence through Bayes' theorem, so a PPV that holds from one site to the next requires a threshold that moves with the site. A higher threshold raises the PPV and lowers the sensitivity, the share of patients with the disease that the model flags. Five methods a receiving hospital can apply, called repairs here, were compared on the study's scores. Each repair states a PPV. Its error is the absolute difference, in percentage points, between that stated PPV and the PPV observed in target patients the repair did not use. The error is read as a function of the number of local labels n, and the sensitivity each repair reaches is reported beside it. Discrimination (AUROC) and net benefit at a 10% threshold are reported per transfer in [`results/ppv_repairs.json`](results/ppv_repairs.json).

### Methods

A transfer is one model applied to patients other than those it was calibrated on. Three receiving hospitals carry the comparison. At Columbia, the four EchoNext models are calibrated on inpatients and applied to emergency and outpatient ECGs (76 transfers). At Beth Israel Deaconess, the models of the seven-source rotation are applied to MIMIC-IV-ECG (28 transfers). Its labels are the ECG machine's own statements, so its observed PPV measures agreement with the machine. At Chongqing, the PTB-XL myocardial infarction model is applied to the hospital's ECGs (1 transfer). Transfers within a hospital are not independent, and the conclusion rests on three sites. Shandong, the other rotation corpora, CODE-15%, the CODE test set and ZZU are a secondary reserve in [`results/ppv_repairs_reserve.json`](results/ppv_repairs_reserve.json). Pairs in which the model is tested on its own source population are in-distribution controls.

For each transfer, the target v is the PPV the model reached at its source, at the threshold that flags 90% of the source's cases. It is computed from the source data alone, before any repair runs, and the file records the digest of that table. The five repairs are:

1. prevalence correction, without labels: the target prevalence is estimated from the model's scores by EM (Saerens et al., 2002), and Bayes' theorem gives the PPV at the source threshold;
2. recalibration on n labels: a logistic model refits the predicted probabilities to the local labels, and the stated PPV is the mean refitted probability of the flagged patients;
3. a refitted threshold: the n labels are split into two halves, a threshold is set on the first half to flag 90% of its cases, and the stated PPV is the share of cases among the patients it flags in the second half, with its one-sided Clopper-Pearson bound;
4. a certified threshold: candidate thresholds come from a grid fixed by the source scores, each is tested from the highest down with an exact binomial test of PPV ≤ v at δ = 0.1, and the lowest threshold passed before the first failure is kept (Learn-then-Test, Angelopoulos et al.); the chance that a kept threshold has a PPV at or below v is then at most δ, and the stated PPV is v;
5. weighted conformal selection, without labels (Jin and Candès, arXiv:2307.09291, Algorithm 1 with deterministic pruning): patients are flagged so that the expected share of false positives among them, the false discovery rate, stays below q = 1 − v, with weights on the model's score that correct for the difference between source and target patients.

The thresholds of repairs 3 and 4 follow a conformal rule, as at the source: among m cases, the threshold sits at the score of the case of rank ⌈(m + 1) × 0.9⌉, counted from the highest score. Labels are drawn by patient, with label budgets n = 25, 50, 100, 200 and 400 and 200 draws per budget. The target patients not drawn form the evaluation set; repairs 1 and 5 use the whole target. Sensitivity is the share of target cases flagged, averaged over the draws in which a repair returned a threshold, then over the transfers of a hospital.

### Results

Without local labels, the prevalence correction erred by a median of 13.1 points at Columbia, 11.4 points at Beth Israel and 51.7 points at Chongqing. It stated less than was observed in every Columbia transfer, and more than was observed at Chongqing. It keeps the source threshold, which flagged 82% of cases at Columbia, 75% at Beth Israel and 84% at Chongqing, below the 90% fixed at the source. The published label-free estimate for Columbia, over that study's selection of transfers, erred by 12.6 points. In the in-distribution controls the correction erred by 2.4 points. Given the true prevalence instead of the estimated one, Bayes' theorem erred there by 0.5 points, against 0.2 points over the published study's EchoNext controls.

Local labels reduced the error. With 25 labels, recalibration erred by 5.8 points at Columbia, 8.5 points at Beth Israel and 9.3 points at Chongqing. The refitted threshold erred by 6.0, 5.7 and 6.6 points. With 100 labels the two errors were 3.1 and 2.6 points at Columbia, 5.7 and 2.2 points at Beth Israel, and 3.8 and 3.5 points at Chongqing. With 400 labels they were 1.9 and 1.6 points, 3.5 and 1.7 points, and 1.8 and 2.6 points. From 100 labels on, the stated PPV was too high about as often as too low, in 0.44 to 0.55 of the draws. With fewer labels, recalibration stated too high a PPV in up to 0.72 of the draws.

Recalibration keeps the source threshold, so its sensitivity is that of the prevalence correction at every budget. The refitted threshold flagged more cases than the 90% it aims for: 99%, 99% and 98% with 100 labels, and 94%, 95% and 92% with 400. When a half holds fewer than nine cases, the rank ⌈(m + 1) × 0.9⌉ exceeds m, no threshold can be placed, and every patient is flagged. The stated and observed PPV are then both close to the prevalence, so the error stays small.

The certified threshold kept its guarantee but was seldom found. At n = 100, the share of draws whose observed PPV fell below v was 0.022 at Columbia, 0.014 at Beth Israel and 0.010 at Chongqing, all under δ. A threshold was certified in 0.168, 0.251 and 0.010 of the draws. At most targets, no threshold of the grid reaches the source's PPV. Where a threshold was certified, it flagged 43% of cases at Columbia, 87% at Beth Israel and 38% at Chongqing, the last over two draws.

Weighted conformal selection did not keep its false discovery rate at Chongqing. There, the share of false positives among the flagged patients averaged 0.75, for a limit of 0.39. At Columbia it was 0.64 for a limit of 0.77, and at Beth Israel 0.46 for 0.60. It flagged 64%, 68% and 86% of cases.

### Falsifiers

The label-free correction would have sufficed outside Columbia if the upper bound of the bootstrap interval of its median error had stayed under two points at both Beth Israel and Chongqing. The bounds were 30.3 points and 52.8 points, so the need for local labels is not restricted to Columbia. Chongqing contributes a single transfer, so its bound reflects the resampling of its patients only.

Certification would have failed under real draws if its violation rate, the share of draws whose observed PPV fell below v, had exceeded 0.15 at n = 100 in a hospital; it did not. The budget would not suffice to certify v if a threshold existed in fewer than half of the draws, and that holds at all three hospitals: 100 labels do not certify the source's PPV at these targets.

### Limitations

The comparison rests on three hospitals, with correlated transfers within each. Beth Israel's labels are machine statements. Each draw's observed PPV is estimated on a finite evaluation set, which adds sampling noise to the measured error and to the violation rate. Repair 5 uses the model's score as its only covariate, so it assumes that the diagnosis depends on the ECG only through that score; the share of false positives at Chongqing suggests the assumption fails there. The target v is the source's PPV, and a hospital that sets another target would see other rates of certification and other sensitivities. Calibration curves are not redrawn here; the calibration of the recalibrated model at 100 labels is in [`results/repairs.json`](results/repairs.json).
